Small precautions before the visit

Treat this as a short guide to the first hour after you notice droppings.

  • Sweeping is the habit to drop first: dried droppings crumble, and the powder is easy to inhale.
  • A room that has sat sealed for weeks deserves a cross-breeze, so prop two openings and walk away for a while.
  • Gloves of rubber, latex or vinyl go on before your hands go near droppings, urine marks or nest stuffing.
  • Soak the pellets and the ground around them, then leave the liquid alone for five minutes or so.
  • Used towels, droppings and any dead rodent all go into one plastic bag, which you knot and put out with the weekly trash.
  • If you feel ill after handling droppings, tell your physician what you were cleaning.
