Months and mice.

Spring cleaning turns up evidence that was there all winter. If you find droppings in a storage box, treat them with the wet method before anything else.

Summer is quieter indoors but busy outside. Burrows, woodpiles and feeders support populations that move toward the house when the weather turns.

Rodents entrench themselves fast, so an early call tends to cost less than patching damage later.

A written estimate is handed over after the survey rather than quoted over the phone, and it separates trapping, sealing, cleanout and repair so that you always know exactly which task each figure pays for.

People who live near Rochelle Park sometimes assume that a clean kitchen is enough to keep rodents away, but animals also come for shelter and warmth, so the condition of the foundation and roofline counts at least as much as housekeeping.

From call to visit.

  • Decant kibble and birdseed into containers with snap-on lids by dusk.
  • Lift the pet bowl at bedtime and wipe down the counters.
  • Hold off on your own traps; scattered ones blur the pattern a technician has to read.
  • Note the day the first sound, sighting or smell showed up.

Small precautions before the visit

Spending a few minutes preparing before you touch anything costs little and protects your breathing.

  • Dust is the hazard, so the plan is simple: wet it down before anything gets lifted.
  • CDC guidance asks you to ventilate a closed area before cleaning, and a half hour is the figure it gives.
  • Single-use gloves suit this job as long as you wash well after taking them off.
  • The surface should look drenched rather than damp before you start wiping.
  • Wipe tools, mop heads and tabletop surfaces that touched the mess with the same disinfectant.
  • If you feel ill after handling droppings, tell your physician what you were cleaning.
