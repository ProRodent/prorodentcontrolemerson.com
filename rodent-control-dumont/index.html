Before the visit.

  • Shut the noisiest room's door and leave it that way until a technician has looked around.
  • Leave dried droppings where they lie, since stirring them sends fine dust into the air.
  • Do not toss bait around, since an animal that eats it may retreat into the framing and die there.
  • Note which room and what time of each scratch, thump or sighting.

Small precautions before the visit

Rodent waste may carry disease, which is why cleanup has to be damp and unhurried. The agencies listed at the foot of this page back up each line below.

  • Never clean rodent waste dry. Brooms, dust mops and vacuum cleaners all throw invisible particles into the room.
  • Stale air in a shut-up crawl space or outbuilding should be replaced before you step in.
  • Anything that mice or rats have fouled counts as contaminated, so wear hand protection and wash afterward.
  • Saturate droppings and stains with household disinfectant, or with bleach diluted as its label directs, then leave it alone for roughly five minutes.
  • Wipe tools, mop heads and tabletop surfaces that touched the mess with the same disinfectant.
  • If you feel ill after handling droppings, tell your physician what you were cleaning.
