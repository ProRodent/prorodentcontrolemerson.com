What happens at the property.

The technician begins outside and circles the house unhurriedly, studying eaves, vents, pipe collars, the footing and the garage door. Each gap and each trace of travel gets noted.

Early signs worth a phone call.

Callers from Montvale usually notice one of these first:

  • A sharp, stale odor in a room kept shut.
  • A trail of droppings along a fence or wall outdoors.
  • Tracks in dust on a basement or attic floor.
  • Droppings on a shelf in a pantry or storage room.

The season matters.

A warm spell in midwinter can move animals around. Do not assume a quiet week means the problem has ended.

Summer storms can knock branches onto roofs, and a branch that touches the house is a bridge. Check the roofline after a major storm.

Before you phone: the property basics.

When you phone from Montvale, the dispatcher will want a picture of the building. These notes are the ones that matter.

  • The attic type: walk-up stairs, pull-down ladder or a small hatch.
  • If the building also holds a shop or office.
  • Whether you have already used traps or bait, and where.
  • Any smell that comes and goes, and the room where it is strongest.

Next steps once you call.

Photos are welcome. Mention them on the call, and dispatch explains how to send them.

If the problem is in a rental, tell the owner or manager. Repairs to the building usually need their approval, and shared walls make coordination worthwhile.

Before the visit.

  • If a car has leaves or fluff under the hood, stop driving it until someone has looked.
  • Bring bags of grass seed, birdseed and kibble inside, off the garage floor.
  • Seal any chewed item in a bag so the technician can study the bite.
  • Find the breaker panel ahead of time, in case a chewed wire turns up.

Before touching any pellet

Patient wet work stops particles from getting into your lungs.

  • Sweeping is the habit to drop first: dried droppings crumble, and the powder is easy to inhale.
  • Sheds, cabins and garages that were shut for a long time want half an hour of fresh air before anyone works inside.
  • Keep your hands covered for the entire job and wash them with soap and water when the gloves come off.
  • Wet the waste thoroughly, wait the five minutes, then pick it up with paper towels.
  • Tie the bag before you carry it anywhere, then take it straight outside.
  • Our expertise ends at the pest side, so anything about your body goes to a physician.
