What the yard is telling you.

Rodents seldom move straight into a house. They arrive in a yard first, where food and cover are plentiful, and the house comes later. A burrow beside the foundation, a path worn through the grass and a heap of seed under a feeder are all signs of a population that will eventually test the walls.

Lots in the borough tend to be compact, which means every feature sits close to the house. A shed, a deck or a stack of firewood can be only a few feet from a basement window.

Things people try that backfire.

  • Filling burrows before trapping, which only moves the animals.
  • Putting out loose bait, which attracts pets and wildlife as well.
  • Piling brush against the house to hide the problem.
  • Leaving the feeder out while a problem is active.

Sheds, decks and the space beneath.

A low deck or shed floor is one of the best nesting spots in any yard. It is dry, shaded and hidden from view. We look underneath with a light, check for droppings and burrow mouths and recommend skirting with mesh or a raised base. For ground-level holes and runways, see rat removal.

Wooded edges and the reservoir buffer.

Some Emerson lots back onto wooded land near the Oradell Reservoir buffer. Woods are a natural source of animals, and a lot at the edge of one sees more traffic than a lot in the middle of a block. We do not claim a specific number, but we do look harder at fences, hedges and wall bases on those properties.

Putting it together.

Yard work alone seldom solves a problem that has reached the walls. It lowers pressure. Combine it with trap setting and sealing, and the house becomes a harder target. A short list of habits keeps the gains: lift feeders at night, keep lids on cans and rake leaves off the foundation each fall.

From call to visit.

  • Unlatch the side gate ahead of time and say if a dog patrols the backyard.
  • Do not trim the shrubs and vines on the wall yet; we want to see them first.
  • Keep a small flashlight handy for dark corners.
  • Capture burrows, pellets and nest scraps on camera before rain alters them.

What Emerson callers ask about yards.

Do bird feeders really matter?

Yes, often more than owners expect. Spilled seed feeds rodents all night. A tray that catches hulls and a habit of lifting the feeder at dusk during a problem both help.

Does the woodpile have to go?

Not always. Raise it off the soil, move it away from the wall and keep it neat, since logs stacked against siding invite trouble.

Will trimming shrubs end the problem?

It helps, but shrubs are one factor among several. We rank them with the others so you can see which chores matter most.

What do I do about a burrow?

Leave it. Filling a hole before the animals are trapped just pushes them to dig another. We handle burrows after trapping.

Do I need to deal with the neighbor?

Sometimes. Fences and shared hedges cross property lines, and a friendly word can help. We explain what is yours to fix and what is not.
