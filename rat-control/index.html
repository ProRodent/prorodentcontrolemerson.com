Why rats start outside.

A rat needs three things: cover, water and a dependable meal. A burrow beneath a shed floor supplies cover, a leaking spigot supplies water, and a feeder, compost bin or open trash can supplies the meal. The house comes later, when the colony grows or the weather turns.

That order explains why we begin outdoors. A rat inside the attic at night often has a home in the dirt twenty feet away, and trapping indoors without dealing with that home only thins the stream.

The outdoor walk.

We look for burrow mouths, which are fist-sized holes with smooth, packed edges and no spider webs across them. We also check for runways pressed into soil or grass, rub marks on fence rails and stacks of lumber or firewood sitting directly on the ground.

  • Holes at the edge of a slab, along a stone wall or beneath steps.
  • Feeders that spill seed, or pet dishes left out after dark.
  • Open-bottomed compost bins and trash cans with loose lids.
  • Wood piles, tarps and stored materials resting on bare soil.

Norway rats and roof rats.

The Norway rat is the common one in the region. It is a heavy animal that digs, prefers ground level and slips into basements through gaps beside pipes. A roof rat, if one turns up, climbs and enters through the attic along wires and branches.

Knowing which one you have changes the trap placement. Ground-level runs call for traps against walls, while overhead activity means working from the attic hatch and the roof edge.

Trapping and sealing a rat job.

Rats are wary of new objects. Traps are often set unarmed for a few days so the animals learn to ignore them, then armed. It sounds slow, and it is why the first night's catch is usually larger than the pattern of a quick, single-night setup.

Sealing follows the catch. A rat can widen a mouse-sized hole in a night, so the metal has to be thick. See sealing with steel and mesh for the materials, and yard and shed work for the outdoor half.

You need not know in advance whether it is a mouse or a rat; describe the signs and we narrow the field.

Close lots, shared fences.

When houses stand near one another, a rat burrow can sit under a fence line that belongs to two owners. If you suspect that, say so on the call. A technician can explain what to do on your side and what is worth a friendly word with the neighbor.

While you wait.

Lift pet bowls at dusk, close the garbage can lid and rake seed from under feeders. Do not corner an animal and do not touch droppings. For the cleanup itself, follow the dampening method laid out on the CDC page linked below.

From call to visit.

  • If a car has leaves or fluff under the hood, stop driving it until someone has looked.
  • Bring bags of grass seed, birdseed and kibble inside, off the garage floor.
  • Seal any chewed item in a bag so the technician can study the bite.
  • Find the breaker panel ahead of time, in case a chewed wire turns up.

Cleaning in a safe order

Rodent waste may carry disease, which is why cleanup has to be damp and unhurried. The agencies listed at the foot of this page back up each line below.

  • Never clean rodent waste dry. Brooms, dust mops and vacuum cleaners all throw invisible particles into the room.
  • A room that has sat sealed for weeks deserves a cross-breeze, so prop two openings and walk away for a while.
  • Single-use gloves suit this job as long as you wash well after taking them off.
  • The surface should look drenched rather than damp before you start wiping.
  • Double-bag anything wet, dripping or sharp before it goes in the bin.
  • We do not diagnose illness. Anyone who feels sick after exposure should see a clinician.

What Emerson callers ask about rats.

How do I know a rat from a mouse?

Mostly by size. Rat pellets are about as long as a grape seed, mouse pellets nearer a grain of rice. Rats also leave wider gnaw marks and make heavier sounds.

Do rats come from the yard or the sewer?

Most callers find the first sign outdoors, in a burrow, under a shed or in a woodpile. We do not guess about drains. We follow the evidence at your property.

Will cutting back the shrubs help?

It helps, mainly by removing cover close to the foundation. But brush alone seldom ends a problem, which is why the plan pairs yard work with trapping and sealing.

Is it safe to use bait around dogs and children?

Loose bait is not safe around either. If bait ever enters the plan, it sits in a locked, tamper-resistant station and you are told exactly where.

How soon after the first call can someone come?

Dispatch offers the earliest opening. Some days that is the same afternoon, and on busy days it is the next morning.
