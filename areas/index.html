Every page on this list follows the same basic pattern. A visitor can open the entry for a neighboring town, compare its ZIP codes and mileage with their own mail, and then call knowing whether the address sits inside the circle we reach from Emerson.

Mileage in the table is measured between the middle of one ZIP code and the middle of another. A house at the edge of a code can sit nearer or farther than the number printed, and dispatch quotes a real arrival window once your street is known.

Towns are listed from nearest to farthest, which is helpful if you are comparing neighboring places, but it is not a ranking of quality or of how quickly we can arrive, since traffic and the schedule on the day matter more than a decimal mile.

Some readers arrive here from a search for a neighbor's town. For them the table is a quick way to confirm that a given mailing code is on the roster before they phone, which saves a few minutes at the start of the call.

Anyone who manages property in more than one of these towns can book each address separately. The plan, the quote and the follow-up dates are all written for one building at a time, never for a whole portfolio at once, and each visit stands alone.

A town appearing here means only that its ZIP code passed the distance check and sits in our data, so treat the page as a booking aid, and let the dispatcher make the final call about your particular street and building.

People sometimes ask why a place far from the center of a county shows up while a closer one does not, and the explanation is simply that the roster follows ZIP code centers and the coverage file, not municipal borders or road routes.

Because the list is built from measured distances, it can be rebuilt whenever the underlying data changes, and the towns shown today are the ones that passed the check on the date this site was last updated.

Two filters decided the roster. A town had to appear in our coverage file, and an independent measurement from the Emerson ZIP code had to land within roughly fifteen miles.

One note up front. Emerson is listed as 07630, a code the table below leaves out because it lists only the surrounding towns. Hillsdale, the nearest entry, appears under 07642.

Boroughs, townships, cities and the rest.

New Jersey sorts its municipalities into several legal types, and the table shows each. Boroughs are by far the most common. Teaneck, Mahwah, Wayne and North Bergen are townships. Hackensack, Paterson, Englewood, Garfield, Clifton and Union City are cities, Ridgewood and Ridgefield Park are villages, and Secaucus and West New York are towns. The label has no bearing on how we trap or seal.

Bergen County holds most of the list. Passaic County adds Paterson, Clifton, Wayne, Totowa, Hawthorne, Haledon and Pompton Lakes, Hudson County adds North Bergen, Secaucus, West New York, Weehawken and Union City, Pequannock sits in Morris County and Belleville in Essex County.

Dispatch opens with three questions: which part of the building, what hour, and what the noise sounds like.

Range and mileage questions.

What does the mileage mean?

Mileage is a straight line from the middle of one ZIP code to the middle of another. Roads run longer than that, so dispatch gives an arrival window by phone.

My ZIP code is not in the table. Now what?

Ring us with your street all the same. Some codes lie beyond our range, and dispatch will answer yes or no about a visit.

Why does Emerson's own code have no row?

Emerson is our base, so it skips the table. Its code, 07630, is in range by definition.

Does a page cover an entire town?

A page lists only the ZIP codes that passed our checks. Some towns span more codes than we hold, so the code printed on your mail decides.
