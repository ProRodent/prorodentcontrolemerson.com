How we handle the animals and the openings.

A return visit counts what was caught, tests each repair and decides whether any trap should move. If the count is zero and no new signs appear, the job moves to its last stage.

The technician begins outside and circles the house unhurriedly, studying eaves, vents, pipe collars, the footing and the garage door. Each gap and each trace of travel gets noted.

A written estimate is handed over after the survey rather than quoted over the phone, and it separates trapping, sealing, cleanout and repair so that you always know exactly which task each figure pays for.

It helps to keep a short note of what you notice before the visit, naming the room, the time of day and whether you hear scratching or smell something stale, because those details narrow the search much faster than a general description of the problem.

A call about a house in Glen Rock usually begins with a few plain observations, such as where the noise is loudest, what time it starts and whether anything has been chewed. Those answers let the dispatcher decide how much time to set aside.

Phone, schedule, follow-up.

Emergency calls are handled the same way as routine ones: the line is answered, the street is confirmed and the earliest slot is offered.

Call from the property if you can. Sounds, smells and a quick look at the attic hatch make the conversation more specific.

While we are on our way.

  • Block off the stairs to a basement with signs of activity if small children are about.
  • Move woodpile logs, cardboard and paper bundles out of reach for now.
  • Do not run a vacuum over droppings or nest bits.
  • Jot down which doors and windows stood open during the last warm spell.

Small precautions before the visit

Most steps here are about stopping dust from drifting through the air.

  • Dry rags raise the same cloud a broom does, so keep them for other chores.
  • Open each outside door and window first, then give the room about thirty minutes.
  • Hand protection comes first, and a soapy wash comes last.
  • Saturate droppings and stains with household disinfectant, or with bleach diluted as its label directs, then leave it alone for roughly five minutes.
  • Once the bag has gone in the bin, wash your hands again, even with clean gloves on.
  • If you feel ill after handling droppings, tell your physician what you were cleaning.
