Why commercial work is different.

Compared with a house, a business sees extra doors, shipments and foot traffic, plus long stretches when nobody is inside. Stacked cartons in a stockroom, a dumpster out back and a loading door swinging all day each offer animals a route.

The cost of a problem is also different. A single sighting in a dining area can mean lost trade, and rodent droppings in stock can mean losing the stock. The plan has to be steady and documented.

Where we look.

  • Receiving doors, dock plates and the sweep along every exterior door.
  • Dumpster pads, grease bins and the space between bins and walls.
  • Stockrooms, shelving and cardboard storage.
  • Kitchens, prep tables, floor drains and appliance hookups.
  • Overhead tiles, party walls and utility chases.

We mark each location on a sketch of the site and keep it with the log, so the next visit begins where the last one stopped.

The business side of sealing.

Door sweeps, dock seals and wall penetrations are common weak points. Closing them is cheaper than treating the same problem repeatedly. Sealing with steel and mesh explains the materials we use, and a walk-through tells you where your site stands.

Shared walls and shared bins.

In a row of shops or an office building with several tenants, one unit's food storage can affect the others. We encourage owners to coordinate on dumpsters, grease bins and deliveries. If you manage the building, a single plan covering all units usually costs less than each tenant arranging its own.

Hours and keys.

Tell us who can let a technician in and when. If the building is empty overnight, ask for an after-hours slot. Keys or codes are handled under your instructions, and we log entry and exit.

Readying the house.

  • Do not stuff the hole yourself; a cornered rodent just chews a fresh one beside it.
  • Put on gloves for anything grimy and wash your hands thoroughly afterward.
  • Hold the spray bottles back until we have settled on a plan.
  • Tell us which way a surprised animal ran.

Before touching any pellet

Come back to this box each time fresh droppings, stains or chewed bedding show up.

  • Resist the urge to tidy quickly with a broom, because speed and dust arrive together.
  • Let outdoor air flow through the space for a half hour, then come back with gloves and spray.
  • Single-use gloves suit this job as long as you wash well after taking them off.
  • Saturate droppings and stains with household disinfectant, or with bleach diluted as its label directs, then leave it alone for roughly five minutes.
  • Use tongs or a gloved hand to lift a carcass, and seal it in a bag at once.
  • When you see a doctor about any illness, mention the cleanup, since the clinician needs that detail.

What Emerson businesses ask.

How often should a shop be checked?

A quiet retail space may need only a monthly round. A kitchen or a stockroom that handles open food usually needs more. The walk-through sets the cycle.

Do you work around opening hours?

Yes. Many visits happen before opening or after closing, and we coordinate access with whoever holds the keys.

Does the landlord or the tenant pay?

The lease settles it. Rodents ignore party walls, though, so tenants and managers benefit from passing our notes to one another.

Will the notes help with inspections?

Many food businesses keep pest records for inspectors. Our dated notes are a useful part of that file, but we cannot say what any particular inspector requires.

Can you handle a multi-unit building?

Yes. We plan by building rather than by suite, since animals move between units through shared walls and ceilings.
