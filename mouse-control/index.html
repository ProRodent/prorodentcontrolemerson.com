How a mouse gets from the lawn to your pantry.

Most mouse invasions start with a seam that nobody has examined in years: a sill plate that settled, a gap where a gas line enters, a door sweep worn to a ribbon. An adult house mouse can compress its skeleton enough to pass a gap about a quarter inch tall, which is roughly the thickness of a pencil.

Once inside, a mouse does not roam far. Its daily range is small, often a few dozen feet, so the nest is usually close to the food. That is useful for us: the droppings you find in a cabinet point to a nest within the same wall or the same floor, not somewhere across the house.

Activity peaks after dark. The first sign is often a faint scrabble just as the television goes off, from the pantry wall, the furnace closet or whichever garage wall is framed together with the kitchen.

Three clues that settle it.

Pellets are the first clue, and their freshness matters. Dark, soft ones are recent. Gray, crumbly ones are old. The second clue is the rub mark, a dull smear of body oil along a baseboard or pipe where an animal brushes past every night. The third is gnawing: pale, splintery edges on a cereal box, a bar of soap or a corner of drywall.

Together these sketch a route on a floor plan. We follow that route to its starting point, and nearly every time the starting point is a gap. A trap set without the map catches a few animals, while the opening stays exactly as it was.

Setting traps the way mice move.

Mice hug walls and avoid new objects in open space, so a trap flush against the baseboard, trigger end toward the wall, beats one placed out in the open. Locked bait stations may sit in a basement corner or a garage, always out of reach of children and pets.

The first catches show which routes are in use. Closing them follows, with metal flashing, mesh and a compound that hardens, as covered on the sealing page. Plain steel wool is a weak choice, since determined teeth drag it out.

Pantry habits that help.

Dry goods in lidded tubs and a pet bowl lifted at bedtime will not evict a mouse already inside. They do remove the reason the next one stays. Skip the broom around old droppings, since sweeping lifts dust, and follow the damp method in the CDC guide linked at the bottom of this page.

A note for commuter households.

In a borough where some people leave early and return late, a house can sit empty for ten hours at a stretch. Mice treat that quiet as an invitation. If you find pellets on the counter after a workday, note the room and the time and mention both when you call.

What a finished job looks like.

A good result is a quiet house: no new pellets and empty traps on the return visit. If insulation or a wall cavity ended up badly soiled, attic and crawl space cleanout may come next, and chewed cable or batting belongs to repairs after gnawing.

Unsure whether mice are the only visitors? A walk-through and quote names the animal and leaves a written plan in your hands.

Dampen first, then clean

Spending a few minutes preparing before you touch anything costs little and protects your breathing.

  • Never clean rodent waste dry. Brooms, dust mops and vacuum cleaners all throw invisible particles into the room.
  • Sheds, cabins and garages that were shut for a long time want half an hour of fresh air before anyone works inside.
  • Keep your hands covered for the entire job and wash them with soap and water when the gloves come off.
  • Contact time does the work: spray generously, pause, and wipe only after the liquid has had its turn.
  • Double-bag anything wet, dripping or sharp before it goes in the bin.
  • Questions about a symptom belong with a medical professional, who can decide if rodents are involved.

What Emerson callers ask about mice.

How narrow a gap lets a mouse through?

Lay a pencil on its side. A seam that wide is plenty. Check beside plumbing, behind the dryer duct, around door frames and wherever a cable enters the house.

Do mice leave when the weather warms up?

Not completely. Cold weather is the famous trigger, but a shaded wall or a damp basement can draw them in July. The gap you ignore in April can become the doorway by October.

May I scatter store-bought poison meanwhile?

Check with the technician before buying any. Bait may send a mouse into the wall to die, and the odor that results can be a bigger job than the original scratching.

When will the scratching stop?

Cost follows the count of mice and the number of routes they use. After the technician tours the property, you receive a sensible estimate.

Can I phone at 3 a.m.?

A person does pick up overnight. When the next day has an opening, we can book the earliest slot.
