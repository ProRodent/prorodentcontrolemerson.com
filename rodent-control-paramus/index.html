What happens at the property.

A return visit counts what was caught, tests each repair and decides whether any trap should move. If the count is zero and no new signs appear, the job moves to its last stage.

Indoors the hunt covers the attic, cellar or crawl space and garage. Pellet piles, grease smears and chewed edges show where animals nest and which way they cross.

People who live near Paramus sometimes assume that a clean kitchen is enough to keep rodents away, but animals also come for shelter and warmth, so the condition of the foundation and roofline counts at least as much as housekeeping.

Callers in Paramus sometimes wonder whether one visit will do, and the honest reply is that it hinges on the headcount of animals and the count of openings they use, which is why the plan always names a return date before the technician leaves.

If the first visit shows that animals are present, the plan puts trapping ahead of sealing, because closing a house with creatures still inside only moves the damage into a wall, and that is a result nobody wants to pay for twice.
