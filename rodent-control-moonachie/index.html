The season matters.

Snow cover can hide burrows and runways, but it also leaves tracks. A fresh snowfall is a good time to look for paths along walls and fences.

Summer is quieter indoors but busy outside. Burrows, woodpiles and feeders support populations that move toward the house when the weather turns.

Small precautions before the visit

Rodent waste may carry disease, which is why cleanup has to be damp and unhurried. The agencies listed at the foot of this page back up each line below.

  • Sweeping is the habit to drop first: dried droppings crumble, and the powder is easy to inhale.
  • Sheds, cabins and garages that were shut for a long time want half an hour of fresh air before anyone works inside.
  • Keep a gloved hand away from your face while working, then wash both hands well.
  • A generous soak, a short wait and a towel wipe, in that order, is the pattern the CDC lays out.
  • Used towels, droppings and any dead rodent all go into one plastic bag, which you knot and put out with the weekly trash.
  • Stay alert to your own health for a month or so, and let a doctor evaluate any flu-like symptoms.
