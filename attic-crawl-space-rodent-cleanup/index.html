What rodents leave behind up there.

An attic or crawl space is quiet, dark and insulated, which makes it ideal for a nest. Over a season, animals tunnel through batting, flatten it into runways and add droppings and urine along the way. Insulation stained dark or matted flat has lost much of its value.

The mess matters for two reasons. Soiled material can hold waste that becomes dust when disturbed, and the smell can seep into rooms below through ceiling fixtures and attic hatches.

The sequence.

  • Airing: open the space and let outside air move through before anyone works inside.
  • Survey: mark where waste is concentrated and where wires or ducts are exposed.
  • Wet down: wet the droppings and nest scraps before moving them.
  • Bag out: remove fouled material in sealed bags, never sweeping it dry.
  • Treat: apply disinfectant to framing and surfaces and let them dry.
  • Replace: lay new insulation only after sealing has been finished.

The CDC guidance linked at the bottom of this page follows the same order for waste, and we follow it for the same reason: damp material does not drift.

When to wait.

If animals are still active, do not start. New material will be fouled again, and a disturbed nest can send adults deeper into the framing. If you hear scratching, call about trapping and sealing first.

A caution for older houses.

Houses of many ages stand in Emerson, and an attic in an older one may hold wiring, flues or old material that nobody has examined in years. If you suspect something unusual, say so when you book. We work around it and recommend the right trade where the question is outside rodent work. For chewed wire sheathing, see repairs after gnawing.

Till we arrive.

  • If a car has leaves or fluff under the hood, stop driving it until someone has looked.
  • Bring bags of grass seed, birdseed and kibble inside, off the garage floor.
  • Seal any chewed item in a bag so the technician can study the bite.
  • Find the breaker panel ahead of time, in case a chewed wire turns up.

Dampen first, then clean

Nothing here requires calling us first. A county health office would give you much the same directions for pellets found indoors.

  • Resist the urge to tidy quickly with a broom, because speed and dust arrive together.
  • Stale air in a shut-up crawl space or outbuilding should be replaced before you step in.
  • Hand protection comes first, and a soapy wash comes last.
  • Contact time does the work: spray generously, pause, and wipe only after the liquid has had its turn.
  • Wipe tools, mop heads and tabletop surfaces that touched the mess with the same disinfectant.
  • Stay alert to your own health for a month or so, and let a doctor evaluate any flu-like symptoms.

What Emerson callers ask about attic cleanup.

Can I clean the attic myself?

Small amounts, yes, if you ventilate first, wear gloves and a respirator, and wet everything. Large areas of droppings are better left to a crew with bagging gear.

Why must the animals be gone first?

Fresh insulation laid over an active nest is fouled all over again. Trapping and sealing come before cleanout.

Does every inch of insulation come out?

No. Lightly marked areas can be treated where they lie. Soaked, matted or heavily soiled sections are removed.

Is dust from droppings really a risk?

It can be. That is why the CDC advises airing the space, wetting waste and avoiding dry sweeping or vacuuming.

Does the odor eventually clear?

Usually, once the soiled material and the source are removed and the surfaces dry. A lingering odor may mean a carcass in the space, which has its own page.
