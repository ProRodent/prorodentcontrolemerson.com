From first look to final check.

The materials are chosen to resist teeth: steel mesh, sheet metal and a sealant that hardens. Foam or caulk alone will not last.

Traps go on the routes the evidence shows, not in random corners. Each device is recorded, and the plan states when it will be checked.

If the first visit shows that animals are present, the plan puts trapping ahead of sealing, because closing a house with creatures still inside only moves the damage into a wall, and that is a result nobody wants to pay for twice.

It helps to keep a short note of what you notice before the visit, naming the room, the time of day and whether you hear scratching or smell something stale, because those details narrow the search much faster than a general description of the problem.

Photographs, pellet counts and notes about chewed material are recorded at every stop, so that a second technician, an insurer or a landlord can see for themselves what was found and what was done about it later.

While we are on our way.

  • Skip the attic and the crawl space for now; torn insulation up there fills the air with particles.
  • Leave nests untouched, since a startled adult may bolt deeper into the framing.

Before touching any pellet

Treat this as a short guide to the first hour after you notice droppings.

  • Keep every dry cleaning tool out of the affected area, the shop vacuum included.
  • Open each outside door and window first, then give the room about thirty minutes.
  • Keep a gloved hand away from your face while working, then wash both hands well.
  • Soak the pellets and the ground around them, then leave the liquid alone for five minutes or so.
  • Collect all of it in a single sealed bag and carry that bag out of the building.
  • Our expertise ends at the pest side, so anything about your body goes to a physician.
