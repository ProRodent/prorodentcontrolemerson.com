Who picks up.

When (551) 274-5838 rings, the dispatcher wants four facts: your street, the room, the length of time it has been happening and who else lives in the house, children and pets included. Those facts decide what gets loaded on the truck.

Late-night, Sunday and holiday calls get the same live voice. If no one can reach you today, you are told so at once and offered a window you can plan around.

Contents of a visit.

  • A walk from roofline to foundation, then through attic, basement and garage, hunting for the freshest evidence.
  • Traps placed on the paths shown by rub marks and pellet lines.
  • Holes plugged with flashing and woven steel mesh, finished with a layer of cured sealant.
  • A second trip to count the catch, test each repair and book cleanout where it is needed.

The sequence counts. Seal first and an animal can be trapped inside a wall; clean an attic while its entrances stay open and you will be cleaning it twice.

What this site will not claim.

This site carries no founding date, license number, staff photo, award or customer quote. We were never handed any of that in a form that could be checked, and an invented claim only deceives readers. If credentials matter, ask the technician at your door.

We also stop short of promising that rodents will never come back. Tall trees, stone walls, wooded edges and a cluttered garage next door keep animals in the neighborhood. What we do offer is a careful inspection, materials built to last and a repeat visit.

Bait, health and agency rules.

The sale and use of rodent poison is governed by federal restrictions, summarized on the EPA page we link beneath each article. Traps and sealing come first. Should bait ever be proposed, the product name and its placement are disclosed to you first.

Where waste has to be cleared, the CDC sequence is ventilate, dampen, wipe and bag. Anyone who feels ill after contact with droppings should call their own physician; diagnosing sickness is not a pest company's job.

Who comes to the door.

Dispatch books the trip and a technician works at your address. Names and portraits of staff are not printed on this site, and we cite no qualification that we cannot document. Ask who will arrive while you are still on the line.

Shingle work, rewiring and big plaster repairs go to other trades, a point the technician makes plain while touring the property.

Prices and scheduling.

We price once the building has been seen, because a number offered sight unseen is a guess. Sealing, cleanout and repair each carry a separate figure that you may accept or decline on its own.

A same-day arrival hinges on how full the schedule is. Dispatch reports what is open and does not promise beyond it.
