New Milford became a borough in Bergen County in 1922, as far as our sources show. It is roughly 3.0 miles from Emerson, and older homes in a place like this often have more gaps for mice to use.

Why people in New Milford pick up the phone.

When someone phones about a property in New Milford, the opening line is often about:

  • A burrow beside the foundation, a shed or the steps.
  • A nest of shredded fluff wedged in a back corner.
  • Patter inside a wall or above a ceiling once the lights go out.
  • A sharp, stale odor in a room kept shut.

The season matters.

Snow cover can hide burrows and runways, but it also leaves tracks. A fresh snowfall is a good time to look for paths along walls and fences.

Heavy rain can send burrowing animals uphill and toward foundations. A wet week can start activity that looked unlikely a month earlier.

Before the visit.

Before touching any pellet

Most steps here are about stopping dust from drifting through the air.

  • Keep every dry cleaning tool out of the affected area, the shop vacuum included.
  • Let outdoor air flow through the space for a half hour, then come back with gloves and spray.
  • Keep a gloved hand away from your face while working, then wash both hands well.
  • Contact time does the work: spray generously, pause, and wipe only after the liquid has had its turn.
  • Double-bag anything wet, dripping or sharp before it goes in the bin.
  • When you see a doctor about any illness, mention the cleanup, since the clinician needs that detail.
